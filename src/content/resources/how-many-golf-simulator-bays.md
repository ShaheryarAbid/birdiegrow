Bay count is one of the first big decisions a new operator makes, and it sets the ceiling on everything that follows: your revenue potential, your rent, your utilization math, and how much staff you need. Too few bays and you cap your income and turn customers away at peak. Too many and you're paying rent on space that sits empty most of the week. The right number is the one your market can actually fill.

## Start with demand, not ego

The instinct is to build big. The discipline is to build for the demand you can prove. A bay only makes money when it's booked, and every additional bay raises your fixed costs whether it's full or not. It's far better to open with a bay count you can keep busy and expand once you're turning people away at peak, than to open oversized and spend a year covering rent on idle space.

## The typical ranges

Most successful independent venues land somewhere between two and eight bays. A **two-to-three bay** venue is a lean, lower-risk entry that one person can often run, well suited to a smaller market or a first location. A **four-to-six bay** venue hits the sweet spot for most markets — enough capacity to handle groups, leagues, and events without the rent and staffing burden of a large floor. **Eight or more bays** starts to behave like a larger entertainment venue, with the revenue upside and the operational complexity to match; it's a better fit for high-traffic markets or an experienced operator.

## Do the utilization math

Here's the number that matters more than bay count: what percentage of your available bay-hours will actually be booked? A venue with four well-utilized bays makes more money than one with eight half-empty ones — and costs far less to run. Before you commit to a bay count, estimate realistic weekday and weekend occupancy for your market and price. Our guide to [utilization rates](/resources/golf-simulator-utilization-rate/) walks through the benchmarks.

The key insight: adding bays only helps if you can fill them. If your existing bays aren't consistently busy at peak, more bays won't fix your revenue — better [marketing](/blog/golf-simulator-marketing-ideas/), memberships, and weekday programming will.

## Space and cost per bay

Each bay needs enough room for a full swing, the screen and enclosure, and comfortable space for a small group to sit. Beyond the physical footprint, every bay carries a cost: the launch monitor and simulator setup, the buildout, and the ongoing rent for that square footage. Multiply your per-bay cost by your planned count before you sign a lease — the number climbs fast, and it's the biggest driver of your startup budget. ## Build for expansion

The smartest approach for many operators is to open with a conservative bay count and design the space and systems so you can add bays later without re-doing everything.

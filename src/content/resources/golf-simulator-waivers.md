A liability waiver is one of the cheapest forms of protection a simulator venue has, and one of the most overlooked. People swing clubs at speed in your space; a waiver signed before they play is your first line of defense, and it works hand-in-hand with your insurance. This guide is a practical starting point — have an attorney draft or review your actual waiver for your state, because requirements and enforceability vary.

## Why a waiver matters

Even a well-run venue can't eliminate the risk of a pulled muscle, a slip, or a club that gets away from someone. A waiver documents that the guest understood and accepted those risks before playing. It won't cover gross negligence on your part — you still need to keep the space safe — but it meaningfully reduces your exposure and signals to your insurer that you're managing risk responsibly. Pair it with proper [business insurance](/resources/golf-simulator-business-insurance/) for real coverage.

## What to include

A solid simulator waiver generally covers the assumption of risk (the guest acknowledges golf and physical activity carry inherent risk), a release of liability, and basic personal information tying the signature to a real person and date. Many venues also fold in a photo/media release, house rules, and — increasingly — consent for SMS and email communication so you can legally text booking confirmations and offers. Keep the language clear and readable; an intimidating wall of legalese gets skimmed and signed without understanding, which can undermine its purpose.

## Minors need extra care

If you allow players under 18, a parent or guardian must sign on their behalf. Build this into your process explicitly — a minor's own signature typically isn't enforceable, so a booking that includes kids should route to an adult signer. Your booking flow should make this the default, not an afterthought at the counter.

## Collect it without friction

The operational challenge isn't writing the waiver — it's getting every guest to sign one without turning check-in into a bottleneck. Paper waivers get lost, skipped on busy nights, and are painful to store and search. The better approach is a digital waiver signed as part of the [booking flow](/golf-simulator-booking-software/), before the customer ever arrives. When the waiver is captured at reservation — tied to the customer record, timestamped, and stored automatically — you're protected on every visit without a staffer chasing signatures. For unattended and [24/7 operation](/blog/golf-simulator-door-access/), a digital waiver isn't optional; it's the only way to ensure everyone who lets themselves in has signed.

## Keep records you can actually find

A waiver only helps if you can produce it. Digital waivers tied to each customer and booking mean that if an incident ever occurs, the signed document is a search away — not buried in a filing cabinet. That searchability is part of why running waivers through your management system beats a stack of paper.

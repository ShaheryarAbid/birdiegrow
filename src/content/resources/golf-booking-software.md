"Golf booking software" means very different things depending on what you run. A public course selling tee times, a private club managing members, and an indoor simulator venue renting bays by the hour all need booking software — but the systems that serve them well are not the same. Buying the wrong category is the most common (and most expensive) mistake operators make, because you only discover the mismatch after you've migrated your customers onto it.

This guide breaks the category down by venue type so you can shortlist the right tools, not just the most-advertised ones.

## What golf booking software actually does

At its core, any golf booking software handles the same jobs: it shows real-time availability, takes reservations online, captures payment, and sends confirmations. The differences — and they are big — live in what it understands about *your* business: an 18-hole tee sheet, a member roster, or a room full of hourly simulator bays.

## Golf course & club software (tee-time booking)

Course and club software is built around the tee sheet. If you run a public course, semi-private club, or municipal facility, you're looking for tee-time booking, dynamic green-fee pricing, cart and caddie management, member billing, and often a pro-shop point of sale. This is also what most people mean by "golf club software" — a system that runs memberships, dues, events, and tee times for a traditional club. Tools like Whoosh, Lightspeed Golf, foreUP, and Chronogolf compete here.

If a tee sheet is the center of your operation, start in this category. Simulator-first tools will feel thin for you, and vice versa.

## Simulator & indoor golf booking software (bay-based)

Indoor golf simulator venues don't sell tee times — they sell hourly bay rentals, and that changes the software you need. You want bay-based scheduling with real-time availability, hour-bank memberships, weekday vs. weekend pricing windows, group and add-on bookings, and (increasingly) 24/7 unattended access so off-peak hours still generate revenue. Generic appointment tools like Calendly, Acuity, or Square Appointments can't model bay-based scheduling or membership hour banks, so operators end up building fragile workarounds.

## What to look for, whatever you run

- **It models your unit of sale.** Tee times for a course, bays-by-the-hour for a simulator venue. If the software can't natively represent how you sell time, keep looking.
- **Payment is built in, not bolted on.** Deposits or full payment captured at booking is the single biggest lever on no-shows.
- **Memberships are first-class.** Recurring billing, hour banks, and advance-booking windows should be native, not a spreadsheet on the side.
- **It reduces staff hours.** Automated confirmations, reminders, and — for simulator venues — unattended door access let labor track revenue instead of sitting idle.
- **You can see utilization.** The number that decides profitability is how full your tee sheet or bays run. If the software can't show you, it can't help you fix it.

## Golf club software vs. simulator management software

If you manage a traditional club, "golf club software" and its tee-sheet tooling is your lane. If you run an indoor simulator venue, the equivalent is [golf simulator management software](/golf-simulator-management-software/) — one platform for bay booking, memberships, payments, customer communication, and revenue reporting, built around hourly bays instead of a tee sheet. Both are "golf booking software"; only one will fit how you actually make money.

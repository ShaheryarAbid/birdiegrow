Insurance isn't the exciting part of opening a simulator venue, but it's the part that protects everything else you're building. You have customers swinging clubs at speed, expensive equipment, and often food or alcohol on site — a combination that carries real risk. The goal of this guide is to help you have an informed conversation with a licensed insurance agent, not to replace one. Coverage needs vary by state, landlord, and business, so treat this as a starting checklist.

## General liability

This is the foundation. General liability covers third-party bodily injury and property damage — the customer who tweaks a back swinging too hard, the guest who slips, the club that flies out of a hand. For a business where people are physically active in your space, this is non-negotiable, and your landlord's lease will almost certainly require it with a minimum coverage amount.

## Property and equipment coverage

Your buildout, furnishings, and — critically — your simulator equipment represent a large investment. Property insurance covers damage to the space and its contents from events like fire or water damage. Because launch monitors, projectors, screens, and computers are both expensive and central to your revenue, make sure your equipment is properly valued in the policy. If a bay goes down, you're losing bookings until it's repaired or replaced, so ask your agent about business-interruption coverage too.

## Liquor liability

If you serve alcohol — even beer and wine — you almost certainly need liquor liability coverage, and you'll need the proper licensing to serve at all. This is a common area where operators underestimate both the cost and the requirement. If you're planning a bar component, raise it with your agent early because it materially changes your risk profile and premiums.

## Workers' compensation and employment coverage

Once you have employees, most states require workers' compensation insurance. If you're running lean with mostly [automated operations](/blog/golf-simulator-staffing/) and few staff, your exposure is smaller, but the requirement typically kicks in as soon as you hire. Confirm your state's rules with your agent.

## Cyber and payment considerations

You're collecting customer contact information and processing payments through your [booking system](/golf-simulator-booking-software/), which means you're handling sensitive data. Using reputable, secure payment processing reduces your risk, and some operators add cyber liability coverage as the business grows. Ask whether it makes sense for your situation.

## Waivers are not optional

Insurance and liability waivers work together. A well-drafted waiver, signed by every player before they swing, is a first line of defense that your general liability coverage backs up. The practical challenge is collecting waivers without creating friction at check-in. Building the waiver into your [booking flow](/golf-simulator-management-software/) — so it's signed digitally when a customer reserves, not scrambled for at the door — means you're protected on every booking without slowing anyone down. Have an attorney draft or review your waiver for your state.

## Talk to a professional

The right combination and coverage levels depend on your specific business, location, and services. Work with a licensed commercial insurance agent who understands entertainment or recreation venues, and revisit your coverage as you add services like a bar, events, or additional locations. This guide is a starting point for that conversation, not a substitute for professional advice.

## Description (paste in full — this is the part that gets parsed)

Most successful independent golf simulator venues land between two and eight bays, and four to six
is the sweet spot for most markets.

Two to three bays is a lean, lower-risk entry that one person can often run — well suited to a
smaller market or a first location. Four to six hits the sweet spot: enough capacity for groups,
leagues and events without the rent and staffing burden of a large floor. Eight or more begins to
behave like a larger entertainment venue, with the revenue upside and the operational complexity to
match, which suits a high-traffic market or an experienced operator.

The more important number is utilization — the percentage of your available bay-hours that actually
get booked. A venue with four well-utilized bays makes more money than one with eight half-empty
ones, and costs far less to run. Every additional bay raises your fixed costs whether it is full or
not, so adding bays only helps if you can fill them. If your existing bays are not consistently busy
at peak, more bays will not fix your revenue; marketing, memberships and weekday programming will.

The practical approach is to open with a conservative bay count, lay the space out so an expansion is
straightforward, and choose software that scales from two bays to twelve without a migration.
